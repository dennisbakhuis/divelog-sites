Angelita — *little angel* — is not a cave. It is an open sinkhole about **60 m** deep with no known
connection to any other cenote, and that isolation is the reason it looks the way it does.

At **30 m** there is a layer of **hydrogen sulphide**, formed where organic matter decomposing at
the bottom meets the boundary between the fresh water above and the salt water below. Because no
water flows through Angelita, nothing disturbs the layer, and it sits as a dense, opaque, grey-green
cloud with a flat surface. Dead trees and branches that have fallen in over the years stand up
through it, so from above it resembles a river with a forest on its bank. Inside the cloud it is
dark, acidic and smells of rotten eggs, and the water below it is anoxic.

The position used is the OpenStreetMap one (OSM node/4412196992); another published marker places
this cenote 3.7 km away.
