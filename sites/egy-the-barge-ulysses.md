**Bluff Point** is the eastern tip of Gubal Seghir, named for the turbulence the current makes as
it wraps round the island's wall. It is a standard overnight mooring on the northern wreck route,
and it has two wrecks close inshore.

## The Ulysses

A 91 m British steamer that left Suez on 16 August 1887 and in the early hours put herself onto the
reef just north of Bluff Point. Help did not reach her until the 20th, by which time the reef had
opened her up and she was abandoned. She lies with her bow in the shallows and her stern at about
27 m, and after nearly 140 years she is more coral than steel — the hull and what remains of the
deck structures are a hard-coral garden with a ship-shaped skeleton inside it.

## The Barge

A small British barge or lighter lying on the slope in the sheltered bay, about 300 m south-west
of the Bluff Point light, from about 5 m down to 25 m. It is unremarkable by daylight and extraordinary after dark: the broken-open hull is
a permanent hunting ground, and the site is reliably good for **Spanish dancers**, moray eels out
of their holes, lionfish working the torch beams, crocodilefish and stonefish. Most boats dive it
as the night dive of the trip.
