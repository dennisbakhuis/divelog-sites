The Inland Sea at **Dwejra** is a small lagoon, a few hundred metres across and only a few metres
deep, behind the coastal cliffs, with a shingle beach and fishing boats pulled up on it. It is
connected to the sea by a single **tunnel through the cliff**, about 80 m long and just wide enough
for a small boat.

The tunnel is the dive. Divers kit up on the beach, swim or are ferried into the tunnel, and pass
through it at 5–10 m in gloom, with rock on both sides and overhead. It then opens at about **26 m**
onto a sheer wall that drops past 50 m, facing the open Mediterranean. The contrast between the two
halves of the dive is why it appears on many lists of the best dives in Europe.

In seventeenth- and eighteenth-century sources the tunnel mouth itself was called *Tieqa Żerqa* —
the blue window — a name the natural arch up the coast later inherited.
