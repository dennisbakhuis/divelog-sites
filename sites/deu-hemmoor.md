## The pit

Chalk was dug at Hemmoor from **1862 to 1976** to feed the cement works beside it. When the digging
stopped and the pumps came off, groundwater filled the hole, and by **1982** there was a lake.

The original excavation went down about 130 m. Rubble from the demolished cement factory was tipped
back into it, which brought it up to roughly **60 m** — still the **third-deepest body of water in
northern Germany**, in a country with no mountains to put deep lakes in.

## What is in it

Thirty-three hectares of water taking about **35 000 dives a year**, and the bottom has been
furnished accordingly: vehicles, industrial machinery, the remains of the factory, and a **Piper
PA-28** that once belonged to the astronaut **Alan Shepard**. There is also an **underwater
postbox** on the bottom, which is emptied weekly and the letters posted for real.

## The other thing about Hemmoor

It kills people. Six divers died there between 2010 and 2016; between 2017 and 2022 there were
fourteen accidents and four more deaths, with further fatalities in 2023 and 2025. The reasons are
the usual ones for a deep, cold, clear inland lake: the visibility invites you down, the water is
4 °C at depth, and 60 m in fresh water with no decompression planning is fatal in exactly the way
60 m anywhere else is. Redundant gas is mandatory below a set depth.
