## The identity problem

Most dive guides call this the **Chrisoula K**: built at Lübeck in 1954 as the *Dora Oldendorf*,
98 m, 3 720 tons, lost on Abu Nuhas on 31 August 1981 carrying 3 700 tonnes of Italian tiles to
Jeddah.

There is a decent case that it is not. A second tile carrier, the **Marcus**, went onto the same
reef in May 1978 with the same cargo, and the physical evidence on the wreck points her way: the
state of the bow, the position of the anchor chain, and — the detail that is hard to argue with —
the **serial number on the engine**, which belongs to the *Marcus*. The most likely reading is that
the *Chrisoula K* struck, came off and sank elsewhere, and that the ship lying here under her name
is the *Marcus*.

OpenStreetMap carries it as the Chrisoula K.

## The dive

Whoever she is, she lies bow-first into the reef with her stern down the slope, 4 m to 27 m. The
hold is full of stacked, palletised floor tiles, still in courses, which is an oddly domestic thing
to find 25 m down. The engine room is open and large.
