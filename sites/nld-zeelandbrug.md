The **Zeelandbrug** crosses the Oosterschelde between Schouwen-Duiveland and Noord-Beveland: 5 022 m
long, opened in **1965**, and at the time the longest bridge in Europe. It is not part of the Delta
Works — it was built to replace the ferry, while the Delta plan was still arguing about whether to
dam the estuary at all.

The dive is on the north side, among the concrete pillars in 10–20 m. The pillars are colonised
top to bottom — dead man's fingers, anemones, sponges, sea squirts — and they concentrate everything
that lives in the estuary into a small, findable area. Lobsters and conger eels live in the rock
armour at their feet.

It is a **slack-water dive**: the current through the Oosterschelde runs hard and the tide tables
decide when a dive is possible.

## Sepia season

For a few weeks in **April and May**, common cuttlefish — *Sepia officinalis* — come into the
Oosterschelde in numbers to mate and lay their eggs, and the Zeelandbrug is the best-known place in
the Netherlands to see them. The Dutch diving calendar bends around it.
