## The ship

Built by **Gourlay Brothers** at Dundee and launched on **3 May 1908**, she ran for
**Österreichischer Lloyd** on the Adriatic coastal service between Trieste and Kotor — a handsome,
modern passenger ship on a comfortable route.

## The sinking

On **13 August 1914**, ten days into the war, she was carrying passengers and supplies north
towards Trieste. The Austro-Hungarian Navy had just laid defensive minefields across the approaches
to **Pula**, its main base, and the ship's officers had been told at a naval conference exactly
where they were.

The captain was not on the bridge. **Second Officer Tenze** had taken the watch without his
knowledge, disregarded the warnings, and put the ship into the minefield **at full speed**. She
struck two mines in quick succession and sank in minutes.

**One hundred and twenty-seven people died**, most of them women, children and crew. Destroyers
pulled 159 survivors out of the water. Captain Winter and First Officer Luppis were put under house
arrest in Pula and found responsible by an Admiralty court.

## The dive

She lies on her keel in **28–40 m** off Rovinj, largely intact and unmistakably a passenger ship —
the promenade deck, the davits, the portholes all still read. She was entered on the **Register of
Croatian Cultural Monuments in 1995**, which makes her a protected site: nothing may be taken, and
she is also a grave.
