This one is a genuine oddity, and it is entirely artificial.

A dam across the **Brielse Gat** in 1950 cut the Brielse Maas off from the North Sea. A **second dam
in 1966** closed what was left, and the sand dug out of the resulting basin was used to build the
**Maasvlakte** — the industrial platform of terminals and power stations that Rotterdam's port
stands on. What remained was a 2.7 km² lake, 40 m deep, sitting in the old river mouth.

The water is **brackish**, half fresh and half salt, and it is deliberately kept that way: salt water
is pumped in from the **Beerkanaal** to raise the salinity, which improves the visibility and
supports a specialised ecosystem that exists almost nowhere else.

Because it *was* the mouth of the Brielse Maas, there are **wrecks on the bottom** from when it was
still a river. Because it is 40 m deep and unusually clear for Dutch water, it is one of the country's
most important training grounds — this is where people do their deep and technical work without
leaving the Netherlands.
