## The ship

Launched 14 December 1872 at C. Mitchell & Co., Newcastle upon Tyne, for the owner W. Milburn:
81.6 m, about 1 600 tons, a two-cylinder steam engine good for eight knots, and a full sailing rig
to go with it — the standard arrangement for a long-haul trader in the decade after the Suez Canal
opened.

## The wreck

She left Liverpool in January 1876 with steel and timber for Bombay, sold the cargo, and loaded
spices, cotton and muslin for the return. On **25 April 1876**, working up the Gulf of Suez,
Captain Care put her position wrong and ran her onto the reef south of Beacon Rock, on the edge of
what is now Ras Muhammad National Park.

She sat on the reef for fourteen hours, came off — and immediately rolled over and sank in 25 m.
The crew of twenty-five were taken off by local fishermen; nobody died. The Board of Trade inquiry
found Captain Care negligent and suspended his master's certificate for a year.

## The dive

Rediscovered in 1977, and known to local fishermen long before. She lies **upside down**, stern
deep at about 30 m and bow up at 18 m, with the propeller and rudder standing clear of the seabed
at the deep end — the classic photograph. Three large holes in the hull let you swim the length of
the inverted interior past the boilers, which is dark, roomy and completely full of glassfish.
