## The ship

Laid down 30 January 1862 at Samuda Brothers, Cubitt Town, on the Isle of Dogs; launched 6 December
1862; completed April 1863. She was a **composite** ship — iron frames planked with wood — 90 m
long, 1 776 tons, square-rigged with a four-cylinder compound steam engine for about 12 knots, and
berths for 250 passengers. P&O ran her on the Suez–Bombay leg in the years before the canal opened,
when passengers and mail for India crossed Egypt overland and picked up a second ship at Suez.

## The wreck

She left Suez on 12 September 1869 for Bombay with passengers, mail, cotton, wine, soda water and
£40 000 in gold. In the small hours of the 13th, in clear weather, she ran onto Sha'ab Abu Nuhas.

She was not badly holed and she was not sinking quickly. Captain P. B. Jones judged it safer to
stay aboard than to put people into boats, and told them rescue was coming. They stayed. At about
two in the morning on the 14th the water reached the boilers and she lost all power, and shortly
afterwards, after thirty-four hours aground, she broke in half. **Thirty-one people died** — a
death toll produced almost entirely by the decision to wait.

The gold was salvaged within two months. The Suez Canal opened five weeks after she sank, and the
route she was built for stopped existing.

## The dive

Rediscovered in May 1984. She lies on her port side at 16–27 m, broken across the middle. Because
the wooden planking has rotted away and only the iron frames remain, you swim *through* the hull
between the ribs with the light coming down in bars — the most photographed thing on Abu Nuhas.
Wine bottles and soda-water bottles still come out of the sand.
