Gozo's Blue Hole, not the one in Dahab, is a roughly circular shaft about 10 m across, worn through
the rock platform at **Dwejra** on the west coast. Divers climb down to it over the rocks, enter,
and descend the shaft to about 15 m, where an **archway** in its seaward side leads out onto the
open wall at around 26 m. The wall drops well past 50 m.

Just outside is **the Chimney**, a fissure in the rock that one diver at a time can swim up through,
following the light, and which opens into the **Coral Gardens** at about 6 m.

## The arch that was there

Directly above the Blue Hole stood the **Azure Window** — a natural limestone arch 28 m high with a
span of about 25 m, formed over something like five hundred years as wave action cut a notch, then a
joint, then a cave, then an arch. Divers leaving the Blue Hole surfaced or ascended under it.

On the morning of **8 March 2017**, in a storm, the supporting pillar failed and the entire arch
went into the sea. Nothing of it shows above water. The rubble is now a dive site in its own right.
