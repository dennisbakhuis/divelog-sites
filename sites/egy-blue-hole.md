## The hole

A near-circular submarine sinkhole in the fringing reef 10 km north of Dahab, just over **100 m**
deep. It is separated from the open Gulf of Aqaba by a reef wall, and connected to it in two ways:
a shallow **saddle** at about 6 m that you can swim over, and **the Arch** — a tunnel through the
wall, 26 m long, with its ceiling at **55 m** and its seaward mouth opening onto a wall that drops
past 1 000 m.

## Why it kills people

Estimates of the death toll range from 130 over fifteen years to 200 in total, and it is routinely
called the most lethal dive site on Earth. Almost all of it happens at the Arch, and the mechanism
is depressingly consistent:

- The tunnel's ceiling is at 55 m, but divers who misjudge the entry find themselves at 70 m or
  deeper before they find the opening, deep in nitrogen narcosis.
- The Arch looks much shorter than it is. Twenty-six metres of rock overhead is a long way when
  you are narked and your gas consumption has doubled.
- Current tends to set *into* the hole, so the swim out is the hard half.
- And it is a shore dive with no gatekeeper. You can walk in off the beach with a single cylinder
  and no plan.

The best-documented diving death anywhere happened here: **Yuri Lipski**, a 22-year-old
Russian-Israeli instructor, lost buoyancy control on 28 April 2000 and died at 115 m with a helmet
camera running. Above the entry point there is a line of memorial plaques cemented to the rock.

## The dive

None of that applies to the ordinary dive, which is why the memorials are so jarring. You enter
over the saddle, the wall inside the hole is covered in hard and soft coral, and it is a pleasant,
easy, beautifully lit circuit — provided you stay above the Arch and treat the blue space below
you as scenery.
