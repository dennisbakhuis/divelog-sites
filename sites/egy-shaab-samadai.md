Sha'ab Samadai is a horseshoe reef about 8 km offshore from Marsa Alam, roughly 4 km² in area, with
a shallow sheltered lagoon inside the arms. A large group of **spinner dolphins** uses that lagoon
during the day — spinners feed offshore at night and rest in shallow protected water in daylight,
and this is one of their resting places.

By the early 2000s it was being visited by so many boats that the animals were being pushed out of
the site they came there to rest in. In **2004** the reef was declared a managed zone and split
three ways:

- **Zone A** — the inner lagoon, dolphins only, no people at all
- **Zone B** — snorkelling
- **Zone C** — diving and boat moorings

It worked, and it is now cited as a model for the rest of the Red Sea. The diving in Zone C is good
on its own account: coral pinnacles, caves and tunnels on the outer reef in 5–30 m.
