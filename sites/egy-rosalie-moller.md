## The ship

Launched as the **Francis** at Barclay Curle on the Clyde in January 1910, and — in the way of
things — built to much the same pattern as the *Thistlegorm*, so the two are often called sister
ships. In 1931 she was bought by the Moller Line of Shanghai, a shipping family of Swedish origin
who named their vessels after members of the family, and she became the *Rosalie Moller*. She spent
the 1930s carrying coal on the China coast.

The name is spelled without an umlaut in the ship registers, and that is how it is written here.

## The sinking

Requisitioned for the war, she carried 4 680 tons of best Welsh coal from Cardiff to Alexandria,
round the Cape like everything else. She reached the Gulf of Suez and was told to wait at Safe
Anchorage H, off Gubal Seghir.

She was still there on the night of **7/8 October 1941**, two nights after the *Thistlegorm* had
been hit forty miles south. The same Heinkels came back. One bomb went into No. 3 hold at 00:45;
she sank at 01:40. Two of the crew were never found.

## The wreck

She sits upright and almost undamaged in 35–50 m, deeper and darker than the *Thistlegorm* and with
nothing in her holds but coal. That is precisely why people who have dived both often prefer her:
no crowds, no torn-open cargo decks, just a complete 1910 steamer standing on the bottom with her
masts up, in current, in the dark. Glassfish fill the superstructure.
