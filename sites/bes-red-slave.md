Red Slave is at the **southernmost point of Bonaire**, where the leeward coast ends and the water
from both sides of the island meets. That geography is the whole site: it is the only shore dive on
Bonaire with serious current, it is exposed to the trade winds, and the entry and exit have to be
timed. The current brings in eagle rays, turtles and big pelagics that do not come to the sheltered
west coast.

## Above the waterline

The name comes from the salt pans behind the beach. In **1837** four **obelisks** were raised along
this coast, colour-coded red, white, blue and orange, each marking a different pan and its loading
point so that salt ships could find the right one. In **1850** rows of stone huts about 1.5 m high
were built beside them to house the **enslaved people** who worked the pans — small enough that two
men shared one, and they walked back to Rincon, at the other end of the island, at the end of the
week. Slavery was abolished in the Dutch Antilles in **1863**, thirteen years after the huts went
up. They are still standing beside the parking area for the dive.
