Gabr el Bint — *the girl's grave* — lies well south of Dahab with no road to it. It is reached by
boat, by 4×4 over the desert, or on a camel, and the hour or two that takes is the reason the coral
is in the condition it is. It is dived far less than anywhere else on the coast and it shows.

The site has two distinct halves. The **south** side, which divers call the dark side, is a steep
wall dropping to 60 m and beyond, cut by chasms, sandy ravines, caves and overhangs — a serious
piece of water, and the one place on this coast other than the Blue Hole where depth can get away
from a diver. The **north** side is shallower and far more colourful: a forest of gorgonians among
massive fallen boulders, generally worked at 20–25 m.
