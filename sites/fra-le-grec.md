## The ship

Built in **1912** by the Dundee Shipbuilding Company: 53.3 m long, 8.6 m in the beam, 808 tons. By
1945 she was sailing under a **Panamanian** flag, with a **Greek** crew and Greek ship's papers —
which is where the French name comes from. Nobody called her the *Sagona*; she was *le Grec*, the
Greek.

## The sinking

On **3 December 1945** — three weeks and three days after the *Donator*, in the same stretch of
water, doing the same job — she struck a **drifting mine** on her port side between Port-Cros and
Porquerolles while carrying wine. She sank immediately. **Two men died and one was never found.**

Two ships, one month, one cause, four kilometres apart. That is the Var coast in the year after
the war.

## The dive

She lies in two widely separated pieces about 1 600 m offshore, at **35 to 47 m**: the propeller
and bow at 47 m, the deckhouse at 40 m, the corridors at 35 m. The **wine barrels** of her last
cargo are still visible in the wreckage.

**Position corrected September 2026.** This site was previously pinned to **Cap Camarat**, 40 km east, but the *Sagona* lies at 42°59′38.6″N 6°16′44.9″E, east of Porquerolles,
right next to the *Donator*.
