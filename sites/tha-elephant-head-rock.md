**Hin Pousar**, between Similan Islands 7 and 8. Above water a single rock breaks the surface in a
shape that reads, with some goodwill, as a half-submerged elephant. Below it, the site is a textbook
example of what Similan granite does: enormous rounded boulders piled on each other by nothing more
than weathering and gravity, leaving a three-dimensional maze of swim-throughs, tunnels, arches and
vertical canyons.

It runs to 50 m at the deepest, though 30 m covers most of the site. Currents can be strong and they
change direction between the boulder stacks, which is much of the difficulty.
