## The ship

An English **collier** built in **1888**, working coal from the north-east of England to the
Mediterranean.

## The sinking

On **26 July 1893** she was running from Shields to Genoa with twenty-five crew and two passengers.
A light mistral was blowing, so she hugged the coast for shelter and took the narrow passage between
**Porquerolles and Giens** rather than going round the outside.

She came out of it into the Rade d'Hyères and straight into the **French fleet**, at exercise. She
dodged one warship and was rammed by the cruiser **Cécille**, despite putting her engines full
astern at the last moment. She sank quickly. Boats from the *Faucon* and the *Wattignies* picked up
every one of the twenty-seven people aboard.

## The dive

She lies in the middle of the Bay of Hyères, about 4 km from the Cap des Mèdes on Porquerolles, on
sand at **23–26 m**. The hull is thoroughly broken up and the individual parts are hard to make
out, but her **cargo of coal** is still there, spread out on the sand on both sides of the wreck.
The site is sheltered by Giens and Porquerolles, with little current and a depth that gives generous bottom time.

According to fr.wikipedia the site is currently **closed to diving**, because of explosives lying
nearby.
