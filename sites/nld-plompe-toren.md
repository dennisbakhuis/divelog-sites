## The village that is not there

**Koudekerke** stood on the south coast of Schouwen. In **1468** Lodewijk van Gruuthuse bought the
area and built a new church to replace one that had decayed. It did not help: the Scheldt was
shifting its course northwards and eating the southern edge of the island.

In **1581** a new *inlaagdijk* — a fallback dyke, built inland of a coast that is being lost — was
run **straight through the village**. That was the signal to leave, and the villagers did. The
church was demolished in **1583**. Only the tower was kept.

It was kept for a reason. The Westerschelde was not yet the navigable route it became, so every ship
bound for **Antwerp** came past this coast, and the tower was their seamark. A 23 m brick tower of
1468 outlived the village it was built for by four and a half centuries because it was useful to
shipping.

It was restored in 1935, 1962 and 1974, taken over by Natuurmonumenten in 1997, and restored again
in 2019–20.

## The dive

The site is the **Koudekerksche Inlaag** below it — the strip of water between the old dyke and the
new one — on the Oosterschelde shore. Shallow, silty, tidal, and dived on the slack.
