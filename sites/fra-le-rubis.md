## The boat

*Rubis* was a **Saphir-class minelaying submarine**, built at the Arsenal de Toulon, launched
30 September 1931 and commissioned in April 1933: 66 m long, 761 tons surfaced, with vertical mine
chutes in her saddle tanks instead of stern torpedo tubes.

When France fell in June 1940 she was at sea in the North Sea, operating out of Dundee. She kept
going — she joined the **Forces navales françaises libres** and spent the rest of the war laying
mines off Norway and in the Bay of Biscay.

Her record is remarkable. **Twenty-two operational patrols. 683 mines laid. Twenty-two ships sunk**
— the highest score of any Free French unit. In October 1941 de Gaulle made her a Companion of the
**Ordre de la Libération**, an honour given to only a handful of ships.

## The wreck

Struck from the register on 4 October 1949 and **sunk on 31 January 1958** to serve as a sonar
target for the French Navy. She lies **upright and largely intact at about 40 m** between Cavalaire
and Saint-Tropez, off Cap Camarat. The conning tower, the deck gun mounting and the mine chutes are
all still recognisable.
