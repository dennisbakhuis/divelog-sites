Signposted as **Cenote Cristal**, known to cave divers as Naharón, and universally described as
*the dark cave*. The water carries so much tannin off the surrounding marsh that the walls absorb
rather than reflect your light; there are no white limestone surfaces to bounce off, and the
passage swallows illumination in a way that unnerves people the first time.

Like Mayan Blue, it was part of Sistema Naranjal until **28 March 2011**, when the Naranjal–Ox Bel
Ha connection folded it into **Sistema Ox Bel Ha**.

## Eve of Naharón

In the Naharón section of the cave, divers found the skeleton of a woman aged between twenty and
twenty-five. Radiocarbon dating put her at around **13 600 years old**, which makes her one of the
oldest documented human remains in the Americas and considerably older than the Clovis horizon
that used to bound the peopling of the continent.

She was not drowned. When she died the cave was dry — sea level was more than a hundred metres
lower, the passage was air-filled, and people used these caves for water, shelter and burial. The
sea came up and flooded her where she lay, which is the only reason she survived at all.
