## The ship

An iron-hulled British steamer of 78 m, built at Sunderland in 1871. On **22 February 1881** she
ran onto the south-west corner of Shag Rock, on the eastern edge of the Strait of Gubal, carrying coal from Cardiff to Aden.
Nobody was killed. She hung on the reef and then slid backwards off it as she filled, which is why
she now lies stern-down at the west/south-west tip of the reef, on a gentle coral slope rather than
smashed on the top.

## The name

For most of the twentieth century divers knew her as the **Sarah H.** — a misidentification that
stuck for decades and is still in some older guidebooks. Research in **1996** established that she
is the *Kingston*.

## The dive

She lies at **4 to 19 m**, which for a wreck this complete is unusual and very pleasant: no
decompression pressure, no gas anxiety, and enough light to see colour. The bow is upright and hard
coral has grown over the forward section until it is more reef than ship. The boiler and the engine
stand clear.

She is a short hop from the *Thistlegorm*, and northern-route boats often do the two in a day — the
1881 wreck in the sunshine in the morning, the 1941 one in the afternoon.
