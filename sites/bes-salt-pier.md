Bonaire still makes salt the way it has since the seventeenth century: sea water into shallow pans,
sun, wind, and a crop of crystals. The modern works are run by **Cargill**, and the salt leaves the
island over a pier that runs out from the southern coast.

The pier has about two dozen concrete pillars standing in 6–15 m of water, and every one of them is
completely encrusted — sponges, corals, gorgonians — with schools of fish holding station in the
shade between them. The light comes down in blades between the pilings. It is one of the most
photographed structures in Bonaire.

Diving is only allowed when **no ship is loading**, and permission is checked at the salt works.
Tarpon and silversides use the shade.
