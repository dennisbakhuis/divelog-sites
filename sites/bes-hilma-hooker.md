## The ship

Built at Van der Giessen de Noord in the Netherlands and launched on **21 May 1951** as the
**Midsland**. Over the next thirty years she went through five more names: *Mistral* (1964),
*William Express* (1967), *Anna C.* (1975), *Doric Express* (1976) and finally **Hilma Hooker** in
1979 — a sequence of renames that is, in the shipping world, a flag in itself.

## The contraband

In mid-1984 she broke down at sea and was towed into Kralendijk. Drug enforcement agencies were
already watching her. At the dock her captain could not produce registration papers, so she was
searched, and behind a **false bulkhead** the authorities found **25 000 lb — about 11 000 kg — of
marijuana**.

Ship and crew were detained while the authorities looked for the ship's owners. Nobody came. No owner was
ever identified, and with nobody responsible for her she simply rotted at the dock and began taking
on water.

## The sinking

On **7 September 1984** she was moved off the dock to an anchorage — a sand channel between two
reefs, deliberately chosen so that when she went, she would not land on coral. On the morning of
**12 September 1984** water came in through her lower portholes. At **09:08** she rolled onto her
starboard side, and within two minutes she was gone.

## The dive

She lies on her starboard side in about **30 m**, 72 m long, in that same sand channel with the
double reef on either side. The hull is intact, the holds are open, and the superstructure is
penetrable. Tarpon patrol her, and she is thoroughly covered in sponges now.
