## The cave

The Ressel rises on the left bank of the Célé just upstream of Marcilhac-sur-Célé. It is the main
outlet of **Réseau Gramat-Sud**, the flooded network that drains 330 km² of the southern Causse de
Gramat, and it is the most important sump in the region.

The first siphon is about **1 870 m** long and drops past **80 m** — some surveys put the deepest
point at 88 m. That is what makes it an exploration cave rather than a tourist one: the entrance
pool is at the side of a road, and forty minutes in you are deeper than almost anything in
recreational diving.

## The exploration

Between **1998 and 2001** the British divers **Rick Stanton** and **Jason Mallinson** pushed the
Ressel from about 2 km to roughly 5 km, finding shallow air-surfaced lakes and clean passage beyond
the deep first sump that everyone had assumed closed the cave. The gallery between the Lac des
Blocs and Lac T is called the **RJ gallery** after their initials. Stanton and Mallinson are the
same two divers who, twenty years later, led the rescue of the football team from Tham Luang.

## The dive

Short of the exploration front, the Ressel is also an ordinary cave dive, with a large, clean,
well-lined entry passage and a gently increasing commitment with distance.
