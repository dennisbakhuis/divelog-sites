Abu Nuhas is a reef about two kilometres long lying just south of Shadwan Island, on the northern
side of the Strait of Gubal. Its top is barely submerged, it has deep water on both sides, and it
sits squarely in the lane every ship uses between the Suez Canal and the Red Sea. It has taken more
vessels than any other reef in Egyptian waters — at least seven.

Four of them can be dived, and they line up along the north face from west to east:

| wreck | lost | cargo | depth |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Carnatic** | 1869 | mail, cotton, wine, gold | 16–27 m |
| **Giannis D** | 1983 | sawn timber | 10–28 m |
| **Chrisoula K / Marcus** | 1981 or 1978 | Italian floor tiles | 4–27 m |
| **Kimon M** | 1978 | 4 500 t of lentils | 4–32 m |

The south side of the reef, where the boats moor, is an ordinary and rather good coral garden;
divers cross the reef top in the shallows to get to the wrecks on the north side, or the boat
repositions.
