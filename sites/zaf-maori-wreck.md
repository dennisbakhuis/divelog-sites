## The ship

A British steam cargo vessel of the early 1890s, owned by the **Shaw Savill** line and commanded by
Captain G. Nicole, on passage from **London to New Zealand** with a crew of fifty-three and a mixed
cargo of British manufactures — **explosives, railway lines, crockery and water piping**.

## The wreck

On **Thursday 5 August 1909**, in thick fog and drizzle, she ran onto the rocks in the bay just
north of **Duiker Point** on the Atlantic side of the Cape Peninsula.

A boat with fifteen crew made for Chapman's Bay and capsized in the surf; six of them drowned.
Altogether **thirty-two of the fifty-three aboard died** — a death toll that has as much to do with
the water temperature and the surf on that coast as with the wreck itself.

## The dive

She lies in about **20 m** in the bay that now carries her name, **Maori Bay**, a little north of
the *Oakburn* (1906) in the south-eastern corner. She has been heavily salvaged over the years for
non-ferrous fittings, and a great deal of intact **crockery** has been recovered — her ship's bell
is in the Hout Bay museum. What remains is a broken but very legible iron hull in a kelp forest,
with the railway lines still lying in it.
