Sodwana Bay is on the KwaZulu-Natal coast north of St Lucia, inside the **iSimangaliso Wetland
Park**, a UNESCO World Heritage site, and the Maputaland Marine Protected Area. Its reefs are the
**southernmost coral reefs in Africa** — the warm Agulhas Current running down the coast carries
tropical larvae far enough south to keep them alive at a latitude where coral has no business
growing.

The reef complex runs about 50 km offshore and parallel to the beach, and the sites are named for
how far up the coast they are from the launch: Quarter Mile, Two-Mile, Five-Mile, Seven-Mile,
Nine-Mile. **Two-Mile Reef** is the largest and the one almost everybody dives.

There is no harbour. Boats are rigid inflatables driven straight off the sand and through the surf
line, which is the part of the day people remember.

In November 2000 a resident population of **coelacanths** was found in the submarine canyons off
Sodwana, at the edge of the continental shelf — the first ever seen alive in South African waters,
and far from the Comoros where they were thought to live. They are at 100 m plus and
need trimix.
