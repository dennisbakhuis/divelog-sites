German inland diving is built on industrial leftovers, and the two kinds here are quite different.

**Flooded quarries and pits.** The **Kreidesee** at Hemmoor is the great one: chalk was dug there
from 1862 to 1976 to feed the cement works next door, and when the pumps stopped the pit filled
with groundwater by 1982. It is the third-deepest body of water in northern Germany and there is a
great deal in it, from the rubble of the demolished cement factory to a sunken Piper PA-28 that
once belonged to the astronaut Alan Shepard. It is also a place people die: cold, deep, and easy to
get to.

**Slate mines.** **Nuttlar** and **Stiefelgrube Christine** are not caves. They are worked-out
slate mines in the Sauerland and the Upland whose galleries flooded when pumping stopped, and they
are dived on fixed guide lines through square-cut, man-made chambers with the tool marks still on
the walls. No karst, no system, no natural passage — which is why the `system` field is blank for
both.
