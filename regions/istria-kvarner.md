The northern Adriatic is shallow, clear and — for divers — mostly about wrecks. Its life is thin
compared to the Mediterranean further south, but the visibility is good, the water is calm, and the
twentieth century left a great deal on the bottom.

Two wars did most of it. The Austro-Hungarian Navy's main base was **Pula**, at the tip of Istria,
and the minefields laid to protect it in August 1914 claimed the **Baron Gautsch** within a
fortnight of the war beginning — the worst civilian shipping disaster in Adriatic history. Thirty
years later the same waters took the **TA35**, an Italian destroyer turned German torpedo boat,
also to a mine. The **Lina** is the odd one out: a peacetime loss, an Italian timber freighter that
ran onto Cres in fog in January 1914, seven months before the war began.

**Krnica**, on the east coast of Istria, has its own house reefs — the Reef, the Beach, Kebal —
within reach of the wrecks.
