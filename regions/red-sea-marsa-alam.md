Marsa Alam was a fishing and mining village until an international airport opened in 2003, and the
coast south of it is the least developed diving in mainland Egypt. The characteristic feature is the
*marsa* — a drowned wadi mouth forming a sheltered bay with a sandy, seagrass-floored bottom and a
reef across its entrance.

That seagrass is the reason to come. It supports **green turtles** in numbers, and — far more
rarely — **dugongs**. **Marsa Abu Dabab** is the best-known dugong bay in the Red Sea, and it is
now managed: boats are kept out of the bay, the overnight moorings were removed, parts of the
seagrass are closed to divers and snorkellers, and rangers patrol it.

**Sha'ab Samadai**, 8 km offshore, is a horseshoe reef whose inner lagoon is a daytime resting
place for a large group of **spinner dolphins**. Uncontrolled boat traffic in the early 2000s was
driving them out, so in 2004 the reef was made a managed zone and split three ways: an inner zone
that is dolphins only, a snorkelling zone, and an outer zone for diving and mooring. It works;
the dolphins stayed.
