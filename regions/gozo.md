Gozo is a small island of soft Globigerina limestone and hard Upper Coralline limestone sitting in
clear, nutrient-poor Mediterranean water. The combination gives it the two things it is known for:
almost no fish and extraordinary rock. Arches, chimneys, tunnels, caves, and walls that drop
straight off the shoreline into 50 m of 30-metre visibility.

Nearly everything is a shore dive, reached by driving to the end of a lane and walking down a
concrete ramp with your kit.

The centre of it is **Dwejra** on the west coast, which packs three of the island's set pieces into
one bay: the **Inland Sea**, a lagoon joined to the open Mediterranean by an 80 m tunnel through
the cliff; the **Blue Hole**, a limestone shaft dropping through the shore platform and out onto
the wall at about 26 m; and **Crocodile Rock** on the seaward side. Above the Blue Hole stood the
**Azure Window**, a 28 m limestone arch. On 8 March 2017, in a storm, the pillar failed and the
whole arch went into the sea. Nothing of it shows above water now.

The north coast has the wall sites: **Reqqa Point**, the deepest shore wall on the island, and
**Double Arch** off Xwejni, two stacked natural arches in the reef. The west has **San Dimitri
Point**, the island's westernmost tip, with caves cut into the cliff. On the south coast
**Ras il-Ħobż** has the Middle Finger, a pinnacle rising from over 40 m to within 10 m of the
surface, and **Xatt l-Aħmar** is where Gozo's scuttled wrecks lie, including the **MV Xlendi**.
