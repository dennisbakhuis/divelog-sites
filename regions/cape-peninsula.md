The Atlantic side of the Cape Peninsula is cold — the Benguela upwelling puts the water in the low
teens even in summer — and that cold is why it looks the way it does. Nutrient-rich upwelled water
grows **kelp forests** of *Ecklonia maxima*, sea bamboo, which stands up from the rock on a
gas-filled stipe and lies over on the surface, so a dive there is spent moving through a wood.

It is also a graveyard. The route round the Cape was the main sea road to India and Australia for
three centuries, the approach is fog-bound in summer, and the coast north of Hout Bay is littered
with iron steamers that got it wrong. The **Maori** of 1909 is the best known of them.
