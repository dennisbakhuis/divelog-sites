Ras Muhammad is the southern tip of Sinai, where the Gulf of Suez and the Gulf of Aqaba meet the
Red Sea proper. It was declared **Egypt's first national park in 1983**, largely to stop the
building that was starting at Sharm el-Sheikh from reaching it.

The geography is dramatic: the peninsula is a raised fossil reef, and its seaward edge falls away
in walls that go past 700 m. Deep water, two converging gulfs and a permanent current make it one
of the richest places in the northern Red Sea.

West of the point, the reef system of **Sha'ab Mahmud** runs out into the mouth of the Gulf of
Suez. Its eastern end carries a navigation beacon, which is why divers call it **Beacon Rock**, and
the beacon is there for a reason: the reef caught the *Dunraven* in 1876 and she is still lying
upside down under it.
